## Model and supported operations

`resource_measurements::capacity` models supplied volume occupancy, not live CPU
or I/O collection. A snapshot is a stock and may decrease after cleanup. It has
a recording day, occupied KiB and a fixed original volume capacity. A selected
pair gives an exact signed change per day; display decimals are not used for
grading. Projection rounds occupied KiB upward, stops negative projected stock
at zero and never performs deletion. A falling historical average does not promise
continued future cleanup.

Capacity planning uses native observation selectors, quote information, a recording
order day and a free-space reserve. Quote prices are fictional credits. One upgrade
arrives after its explicit lead time, at the beginning of that day before its load.
Each published workload adds a constant daily amount and explicit one-off arrivals.
The finite test checks reserve on **every** day, including before delivery. Equality
with the safe occupancy limit still retains reserve; exceeding it fails. Minimum
headroom can occur before an upgrade, rather than at the end of the horizon.

The task also checks the required measurement period, latest evidence, minimum
window length, quote budget and valid date. Distinct recent windows, affordable
quotes, dates and larger viable reserves can pass. A passing workload never hides
a failing budget/window or another workload. A plan that eventually has enough
space still fails if it ran short earlier. Correcting inputs requires a fresh Test;
changing only the displayed case is read-only.

Bounds:32observations, eight quotes/cases, eight scheduled additions per case,
90future days, finite numeric/text limits and versioned closed data. Execution
is bounded arithmetic in process. No host files, monitoring, shell, network,
services, orders, data cleanup or disk resizing occur. No arbitrary expressions,
continuous collection, multiple/staged upgrades, uncertain delivery distribution,
statistical confidence model or monitoring-product emulation is implemented.
Projected assumptions and supplied workload tests must be labeled separately.
The graph uses line/point styles and text, not color alone; exact data and native
selectors accompany the visual. Accessibility acceptance remains unverified.
